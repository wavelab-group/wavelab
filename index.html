Why we're here

Mission & Vision

Our Mission

To advance the science and engineering of elastic waves by developing predictive models, discovering unconventional wave phenomena, and designing architected materials with tailored dynamic properties.

Our Vision

To establish an interdisciplinary research environment where wave physics, mechanics, and computational design converge to enable next-generation mechanical and acoustic technologies.

What we study

Research Focus Areas

We study the interaction between elastic waves and engineered material structures, developing analytical and computational tools for wave manipulation.

Phononic & Elastic Metamaterials

Tailoring wave propagation through the architecture and mechanical properties of structured materials.

Dispersion Engineering & Inverse Design

Designing material interactions and architectures to achieve targeted dispersion characteristics.

Nonlocal Wave Mechanics

Exploring how beyond-nearest-neighbor interactions create unconventional wave behavior.

Dynamic & Programmable Materials

Investigating tunable wave propagation through time-varying, reconfigurable architectures.

01

Dispersion Engineering and Inverse Design

We develop mathematical and computational frameworks to design dispersion relations with prescribed characteristics — including critical points, band degeneracies, anisotropic and nearly isotropic dispersion, and customized group-velocity behavior.

02

Nonlocal Elastic and Phononic Metamaterials

We investigate how interactions extending beyond nearest neighbors influence the dynamic response of periodic systems, providing additional design freedom for tailoring wave dispersion and propagation.

03

Wave Degeneracies and Critical Points

We study the formation, classification, and engineering of critical points and band degeneracies in wave-vector space, including higher-order dispersion features and propagating/evanescent wave interactions.

04

Programmable and Spatiotemporally Modulated Structures

We explore reconfigurable architected materials and time-dependent modulation strategies for controlling elastic waves, including geometry-driven modulation and dynamic porous lattices.

05

Computational and Data-Driven Wave Design

We combine analytical methods, finite-element simulations, optimization, and machine-learning approaches to accelerate the analysis and inverse design of wave-supporting structures.
